# Durable Workflow Server 3.4.0 (native)

Server 3.4.0 adds opt-in activity/workflow continuations to the native Server.
Its exact images are `durableworkflow/server:3.4.0` and
`ghcr.io/durable-workflow/server:3.4.0`; the published Compose default selects
3.4.0. Rolling aliases move separately after published-image qualification.

## Upgrade

There is no schema upgrade from 3.3.x. MySQL, MariaDB and PostgreSQL remain at
native schema 11; SQLite remains at schema 10. The worker protocol stays at
`1.20` and the control plane at version `2`. Existing clients keep their
ordinary completion/poll behavior and do not need a new SDK release.

For upgrades from 3.2.x or earlier, follow the
[3.3.0 upgrade and rollback instructions](3.3.0.md#upgrade), including backup,
explicit `schema-upgrade` and condition-wait rollback constraints. This release
does not relax those constraints or repair a mixed condition-wait chain.

## Activity/workflow continuations

[PR #1116](https://github.com/durable-workflow/server/pull/1116) adds the
`activity_workflow_continuation` capability and dedicated history/completion
routes. [PR #1118](https://github.com/durable-workflow/server/pull/1118) keeps
the activity result, resumed workflow task, commands and exact-request receipt
under one transition lock and commit.

A compatible worker can replay an eligible run's complete history plus its own
activity result, then submit the next commands with that result. The Server
checks the authoritative prefix, original activity authority, worker admission,
capacity and resumed task. A known eligibility miss restores its savepoint and
transaction caches, then records an ordinary completion. Command, storage,
decoder, invariant and budget faults roll back the entire transition visibly.
The combined history transaction budget includes the activity result.

Exact retries recover the recorded decision; changed requests conflict. Stored
receipts are bound to the completed activity/run and the accepted workflow task.
A declined retry may recover its original still-live carried lease; it cannot
claim or renew one.

The initial capability accepts at most 256 history events and 16 commands:
`schedule_activity`, `start_timer`, `complete_workflow` and
`record_version_marker`, without scopes or parallel groups. Pending workflow or
update work, cancellation, incomplete/untimed history and reference-backed
payloads do not take the fast path. Server-assigned event time remains
authoritative. A speculative worker must abandon the fast path if workflow code
reads business time at or after its speculative result.

Enablement is a separate worker opt-in and requires a matching qualified SDK;
existing SDKs do not enable it automatically. Drain or disable opt-in workers
before downgrading a producer or introducing older serving nodes. The PHP
consumer and published-pair acceptance are tracked in
[SDK #341](https://github.com/durable-workflow/sdk-php/issues/341).
No benchmark speedup or latency improvement is claimed.

## Known limitations

The [3.3.0 known limitations](3.3.0.md#known-limitations) still apply.
Source/backend controls, published-image compatibility and a measured workload
are separate evidence. The broader performance-report acceptance remains in
[issue #1078](https://github.com/durable-workflow/server/issues/1078).
